### camelrun/strong: stalls and rate limits are found in seconds, and the pair fails fast

- **First token:** `camelrun/strong`'s members (Gemini 4 Carbon on flex, then on the default tier) wait for a first token for 30 seconds plus 0.4 seconds per thousand input tokens, at most the runtime's usual wait. This replaces 120 seconds, or 300 at `high`; `xhigh` and `max` keep 300 seconds. Measured on OpenRouter on 2026-11-12, the first token came in 5–31 s up to 60k tokens and 63–71 s at 150k. A member that sends nothing by then has stalled, and the other member takes the request at once (no flex wait after a stall). An agent's own `runLimits.firstTokenSeconds` still wins. Other models are unchanged.
- **Flex wait:** at most 10 seconds (`STRONG_FLEX_WAIT_CAP_SECONDS`) on both backends, down from 30 by default on the Workers backend; the Node runtime, which moved at once, now waits the same. Probed at 60k tokens: on OpenRouter's shared Google quota flex answered every request with an immediate 429; on our own key, 8 of 10, in up to 95 seconds.
- **Both members down:** When both strong members fail and the last is unavailable, the error is exactly `503 Service Unavailable: camelrun/strong has no member that can take this request now (flex and the default tier both failed); it is retried after a backoff`. Member names and errors appear only in the `virtual_model_chain_failed` log. The flex capacity window is wall-clock time from the first failure, including provider calls and backoff. A flex stall moves at once; Workers logs `model_flex_fallback` with `reason: "stall"` (capacity moves use `reason: "capacity"`) and `model_flex_fallback_cost` when the default tier answers. Thinking levels `xhigh` and `max` keep the 300-second first-token wait; `high` uses the input budget. An agent's own first-token limit still wins.
